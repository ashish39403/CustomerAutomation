# Customer Support Escalation Policy

Harbor & Pine sells through Amazon. All Harbor & Pine orders are placed through Amazon; Harbor & Pine does not operate a direct storefront. This policy defines when automation may respond directly and when a matter must be escalated to a human support agent.

## Mandatory Human Escalation

Escalate to a human support agent immediately when any of the following applies:

1. Refund or replacement request above $150.
2. Chargeback, payment dispute, or Amazon A-to-z Guarantee claim.
3. Legal threat, safety incident, injury, or serious complaint.
4. Suspected fraud or repeated abuse.
5. Personal-data request (access, correction, deletion, or export of customer data).
6. Any policy exception.
7. Paddle board safety concern beyond ordinary setup or troubleshooting (for example, seam failure, valve failure, or sudden deflation on the water).
8. Repeated defect after one completed troubleshooting attempt.
9. Shelving structural failure or risk of injury (for example, a cracked or collapsed shelf).

## What Automation May Communicate

Automation may:

- Share product information (paddle board sizing, PSI, shelving variants and finishes).
- Share assembly, setup, cleaning, and storage instructions.
- Explain troubleshooting steps for leaks, cracked panels, missing hardware, and wrong finish.
- List required evidence (Amazon order ID, photos, variant, finish, panel number).
- Explain delivery tracking boundaries and the Amazon-versus-Harbor & Pine responsibility split.
- Explain that remedies are reviewed case by case and are not automatic.
- Acknowledge a request and explain that it requires human review.

Automation must never:

- Promise a refund, replacement, discount, or Amazon action.
- State that a claim is approved or denied.
- Change a delivery address, reroute a package, or guarantee a delivery date.
- Process a personal-data request.
- Make a policy exception.

## What Requires a Human Support Agent

A human support agent is required for:

- All mandatory human escalation triggers above.
- Reviewing damage photos and deciding on replacement parts, panels, or hardware.
- Approving or denying any refund or replacement.
- Handling chargebacks, payment disputes, and Amazon A-to-z Guarantee claims.
- Handling legal threats, safety incidents, injuries, and serious complaints.
- Handling suspected fraud or repeated abuse.
- Processing personal-data requests.
- Granting any policy exception.

## Escalation Handling

When a trigger is met, automation should:

1. Acknowledge the customer's concern.
2. Explain that the matter requires review by a human support agent.
3. Set expectations that a support agent will follow up (without promising a specific timeline or outcome).
4. Collect any available evidence (Amazon order ID, photos) to hand off to the human agent.
5. Route the conversation to a human support agent.

Automation must not attempt to resolve an escalated trigger on its own.
